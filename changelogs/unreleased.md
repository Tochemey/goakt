# Unreleased

## Performance

- **An actor under steady load no longer allocates mailbox segments.** The default mailbox kept one drained segment for reuse, so a backlog deeper than two segments dropped all but one of the segments it drained and the next burst allocated 2 KiB segments again: a `BenchmarkTellPingPong` client with 100 messages in flight allocated 20 B per message, which kept the garbage collector running about 250 times a second. The mailbox now keeps up to 32 drained segments and reuses them oldest first, so that benchmark allocates nothing per message at 100 and at 1,000 messages in flight. The ring of spares is allocated the first time an actor drains a segment it cannot rewind, so an actor whose backlog never exceeds one segment keeps paying nothing for it, and one whose backlog once exceeded two segments retains at most 64 KiB of spare segments.
- **Spawning an actor is about 2.5× faster.** Validating an actor address compiled its name pattern on every call, four times per spawn and twice more per ancestor of a child, which was two thirds of the allocations of a spawn. The pattern is now compiled once. On an 8-core arm64 machine a top-level `Spawn` takes 7 µs, 4.6 KB and 75 allocations, from 17–19 µs, 16.2 KB and 239; a child of a parent twenty levels deep takes 22 µs, from 102 µs.
- **Stopping an actor no longer scans every actor that shares its name.** The actor tree indexed names as slices and removed a stopped actor from its slice by scanning it under the tree's global write lock, so with many actors sharing a name (children of different parents, as in a tree of workers) every stop made every spawn wait longer. The nodes of a name are now a linked list, unlinked in constant time. Creating 2²⁰ actors in a binary tree takes 43 s, from 186 s, on an 8-core arm64 machine.
- **An actor address is validated once.** `Spawn` validated the new actor's address twice, and validating a child's address re-validated every ancestor, so the cost of a child spawn grew with its depth. The result of validating an address is now memoised, as a grain identity's already was, and `Spawn` validates it once. The grain-identity and extension-ID validators now match their precompiled patterns as well.
- **Actors are spawned, stopped and watched in parallel.** The actor tree kept every actor's relationships and its three indexes under one system-wide lock, so every spawn, stop, watch and unwatch of the system waited for every other: eight cores spawning at once managed 1.6 times the rate of one. The indexes are now split into 64 shards with a lock each, and a node's children and watchers are guarded by that node's own lock, so actors in different parts of the tree are created and stopped without waiting on each other. Creating 2²⁰ actors as a binary tree, each actor spawning its two children, takes 10 s on an 8-core arm64 machine, from 25 s with the previous commits and 697 s on v4.6.4, and eight cores spawning at once reach 2.4 times the rate of one. A name lookup, the children of an actor and its watchers are still read under one lock; listing every actor or a whole subtree now reads one shard or node at a time instead of freezing the tree.
- **A spawn no longer allocates a closure per inherited setting.** The settings every actor inherits from its system or parent, the actor system, init retries and timeout, remoting, passivation manager and strategy, metric provider, supervisor and mailbox, were handed to the PID as one closure each. They now travel as one value, which takes a top-level spawn from 62 to 53 allocations and a child spawn from 64 to 54, about 250 B less per actor, with the same settings applied in the same order.
