# v4.6.5 - 2026-10-30

## Highlights

- **Spawning an actor is about 3× faster and allocates a tenth as much.**
- **Actors are spawned, stopped and watched in parallel.** Creating 2²⁰ actors as a binary tree takes 10 s, from 697 s.
- **Actor asks allocate nothing per request and are about 8% faster.**
- **Grain messaging is faster:** asks by about 12%, acknowledged tells by about 20%, one-way tells by about 20% with less than half the memory per message.
- **Actor message throughput is higher:** 82–96 million messages/sec actor to actor, from 60–65 million, with no allocation per message.

All figures are measured on an 8-core arm64 machine; where a figure says "from", the earlier value is v4.6.4's.

## Performance

- **Spawning an actor is about 3× faster and allocates a tenth as much.** A top-level `Spawn` takes 6.4 µs, 2.9 KB and 25 allocations, from 17–19 µs, 16.2 KB and 239; a child of a parent twenty levels deep takes 8.2 µs, from 102 µs. The spawn path compiles the name pattern once, validates the address once, builds the `PreStart` retry machinery only after a first failure, and builds the `ActorStarted` event only for a subscriber. Settings, errors and event delivery are unchanged.
- **Actors are spawned, stopped and watched in parallel.** The actor tree's one system-wide lock is replaced by 64 index shards and per-node locks, and stopping an actor no longer scans the actors that share its name. Creating 2²⁰ actors as a binary tree takes 10 s, from 697 s.
- **An actor under steady load no longer allocates mailbox segments.** The default mailbox keeps up to 32 drained segments for reuse instead of one, so `BenchmarkTellPingPong` allocates nothing per message, from 20–30 B. An actor whose backlog once spanned several segments retains at most 64 KiB of spares.
- **An actor `Ask` no longer allocates its reply channel.** The channel comes from a pool and goes back only when the reply is in hand and the timeout was stopped first. `Ask` and `AskPairwise` go from 128 B to 0 B per request, and `Ask` completes 1.52 million round trips/sec, from 1.41 million.
- **A grain ask or tell no longer parks on a timer channel.** The timeout is delivered into the reply channel itself, as for the actor `Ask`; errors are unchanged. `BenchmarkGrainAsk` completes 1.43 million round trips/sec, from 1.27 million; `BenchmarkGrainTell` 2.5 million, from 2.1 million.
- **A one-way tell to a grain no longer allocates per message under a backlog.** The default grain mailbox is now the segmented queue the actor mailbox uses: one-way messages are stored as values in 2 KiB segments of 32, and other messages keep their own context in the same queue and order. `BenchmarkGrainTellOneWayPairwise` delivers 10.8 million messages/sec at 54 B per message, from 9 million at 137 B. A grain keeps its first segment once it has received a message, until it passivates; one that never has is smaller, 600 B of live heap from 776 B. Systems with millions of resident grains that have each received messages should budget 2 KiB per grain, or give them `WithGrainMailboxCapacity`, whose mailbox keeps no segment.
